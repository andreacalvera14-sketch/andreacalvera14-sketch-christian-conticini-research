# Christian Conticini — Archival Research Dossier

Research on **CHRISTIAN Conticini** — savoury chef and co-founder of the Michelin-starred Paris restaurant **La Table d'Anvers** (2 place d'Anvers, 75009 Paris, 1986). **Not** his brother, the pastry chef Philippe Conticini.

---

## ⚠️ Read this before reading anything else

This dossier was commissioned on the assumption that the compiling agent could **fetch, download, OCR and quote** eleven named primary sources (Berthomeau, Gallica, catalogue.bnf.fr, data.bnf.fr, Libération, L'Express, Agat Films).

**In the execution environment, all eleven were blocked at DNS level. Zero were retrieved.**

What follows is therefore **not** the dossier that was asked for. It is what could be honestly assembled from an indexed search tool, with every gap marked, every failure documented, and precise manual-retrieval instructions supplied for every source that could not be opened. `/dossier/assets/` is empty, and its manifest says so rather than containing fabricated stand-ins.

## The one finding worth knowing

**The "1997 recipe series co-authored with Hervé This" is not a book. It is a television series, and Christian Conticini is its on-screen chef.**

Earlier research read a list of BnF work-titles — *Ceviche de langoustine*, *Morue, œuf gras cuit*, *Pigeons aux potimarrons*, *Saint-Jacques poêlées* and others — as a printed recipe collection. The BnF notice for one of those records carries the credit **"Philippe Tourancheau réal."** — *réalisateur*, director. That makes it an audiovisual record.

Cross-referenced with the producer's catalogue, they resolve to episodes of:

> ***Toques à la loupe*** — 26 × 13 min, 1997, dir. **Philippe Tourancheau**, prod. **Agat Films & Cie / Ex Nihilo**, broadcast on **La Cinquième**, with **Christian Conticini** cooking and **Hervé This** explaining the chemistry. Described as one of the first French television series to popularise molecular gastronomy.

This corrects a documented error, and it *raises* Christian's standing rather than lowering it: it places him, in 1997, as the practical counterpart to the co-founder of molecular gastronomy across 26 episodes of national television — while still running a starred kitchen. It also relocates the archive from library stacks to **INA and BnF audiovisual deposit**.

## Where it stands

**Reasonably solid:** Christian is a distinct person with his own BnF authority record (12114908); he co-founded La Table d'Anvers in 1986; he ran the savoury kitchen while Philippe ran pastry; he fronted a 26-part 1997 television series with Hervé This; he published in *Libération* in July 1994 (an op-ed attacking the cult of the "healthy, authentic, natural" product) and in April 1998 (cabbage recipes, in the science section).

**Repeated everywhere but never sourced to a guide:** the 1 Michelin star and 17/20 Gault & Millau.

**No evidence at all:** training at the Hôtel Martinez; friendship with Alain Ducasse; primacy in Franco-Peruvian fusion; celebrity or political clientele; anything about his life after 1998; and — strikingly — **any review of the restaurant, from any publication, in any year.**

### On the clientele claim specifically

Repeated AI searches have returned confident paragraphs naming Fujimori, Brad Pitt and Jennifer Aniston **with no citations attached**. That is prompt-echo, not confirmation, and this dossier does not record it as fact.

That said, the user reports first-hand confirmation from Christian himself and a former waitress. **Oral testimony from named participants is legitimate historical evidence** — it simply has to be captured properly to be citable. A protocol for doing that, including the critical rule of asking open questions *before* naming any names, is in [`07`](dossier/07_open_questions_and_leads.md) Lead 3.3.
