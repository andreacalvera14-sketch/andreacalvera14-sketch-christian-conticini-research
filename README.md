# Christian Conticini — Archival Research Dossier

Research on **CHRISTIAN Conticini** — savoury chef and co-founder of the Michelin-starred Paris restaurant **La Table d'Anvers** (2 place d'Anvers, 75009 Paris, 1986). **Not** his brother, the pastry chef Philippe Conticini.

---

## ⚠️ Read this before reading anything else

This dossier was commissioned on the assumption that the compiling agent could **fetch, download, OCR and quote** eleven named primary sources (Berthomeau, Gallica, catalogue.bnf.fr, data.bnf.fr, Libération, L'Express, Agat Films).

**In the execution environment, all eleven are blocked at DNS level. Zero have been retrieved, across two separate attempts using independent mechanisms.** The hostnames do not resolve; this is not a paywall, a rate limit or a bad URL, and retrying them does not help.

**What the second attempt did instead — and what it found.** Rather than re-hammering eleven dead hostnames, the second pass went looking for *other carriers of the same information*: different guides, aggregators, institutions and national presses. That changed the picture in three places:

- **A guide review of the restaurant exists, and Christian is its subject.** The first pass concluded that no review of La Table d'Anvers survived anywhere. That was wrong — it was a search failure, not an absence. The record of a chef written out of his own restaurant turns out to be filed under **the restaurant**, not under his name. See [`03`](dossier/03_michelin_gaultmillau_criticism.md) §2.
- **Christian's role in the 1997 television series is larger than recorded** — the BnF classes the records as *image animée* and credits him on **conception**, not as a participant. See [`02`](dossier/02_sources_analyzed.md) Source 15.
- **Family testimony settled two questions the published record could not**: he is the **elder** brother, and he trained at the **Hôtel Martinez, Cannes, under Chef Duparc and then Chef Christian Willer**. Willer's documented 1985 arrival at the Martinez dates that training to immediately before the 1986 opening. See [`01`](dossier/01_biography_chronology.md) §2.

**What has not changed.** Nothing has been read in original form. Every new source above also reached this dossier through an indexed search tool, so the evidentiary floor is still summary, not document, and everything new is tagged **[PARTIAL]**. `/dossier/assets/` is still empty, and its manifest says so rather than containing fabricated stand-ins. The remaining gap is specific and stubborn: **the French-language period press** — *Le Monde*, *Le Figaro*, *Le Point*, *Thuriès* — which is exactly where a real critical record of Christian's cooking would sit, and which needs RetroNews or a BnF reading room.

Full detail: [`dossier/08_source_map.md`](dossier/08_source_map.md).

## The one finding worth knowing

**The "1997 recipe series co-authored with Hervé This" is not a book. It is a television series, and Christian Conticini is its on-screen chef.**

Earlier research read a list of BnF work-titles — *Ceviche de langoustine*, *Morue, œuf gras cuit*, *Pigeons aux potimarrons*, *Saint-Jacques poêlées* and others — as a printed recipe collection. The BnF notice for one of those records carries the credit **"Philippe Tourancheau réal."** — *réalisateur*, director. That makes it an audiovisual record.

Cross-referenced with the producer's catalogue, they resolve to episodes of:

> ***Toques à la loupe*** — 26 × 13 min, 1997, dir. **Philippe Tourancheau**, prod. **Agat Films & Cie / Ex Nihilo**, broadcast on **La Cinquième**, with **Christian Conticini** cooking and **Hervé This** explaining the chemistry. Described as one of the first French television series to popularise molecular gastronomy.

This corrects a documented error, and it *raises* Christian's standing rather than lowering it: it places him, in 1997, as the practical counterpart to the co-founder of molecular gastronomy across 26 episodes of national television — while still running a starred kitchen. It also relocates the archive from library stacks to **INA and BnF audiovisual deposit**.

## Evidence tags

Used consistently throughout.

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[VERIFIED]** | Corroborated by multiple independent sources, internally consistent |
| **[PARTIAL]** | Attested via an indexed summary of a source not read in original form |
| **[UNVERIFIED]** | Asserted somewhere, no source located |
| **[FAMILY TESTIMONY]** | Stated directly by an immediate family member — primary oral evidence, tagged separately only because it is not yet corroborated by a citable document |
| **[COULD-NOT-ACCESS]** | A specific document that would settle the point, unreachable here |

**No claim is tagged [VERIFIED] on the strength of a source the agent did not itself read.** Since nothing was read in original form, [VERIFIED] appears only where several independent indexed sources agree.

## Where it stands

**Reasonably solid:** Christian is a distinct person with his own BnF authority record (12114908); he co-founded La Table d'Anvers in 1986; he ran the savoury kitchen while Philippe ran pastry; he is a credited author of a 26-part 1997 television series with Hervé This; he published in *Libération* in July 1994 (an op-ed attacking the cult of the "healthy, authentic, natural" product) and in April 1998 (cabbage recipes, in the science section); and a surviving guide notice praises him by name for inventing and reinventing flavour combinations, describing theme menus built on novel vegetables and rare spices.

**Attested by family testimony, not yet by a document:** that he is the elder brother, and that he trained at the Hôtel Martinez under Chef Duparc and then Chef Christian Willer.

**Repeated everywhere but never sourced to a guide edition:** the 1 Michelin star and 17/20 Gault & Millau — and the one guide page actually located carries **16/20**, not 17. That conflict is unresolved and is set out at [`03`](dossier/03_michelin_gaultmillau_criticism.md) §2.1 rather than smoothed away.

**No evidence at all:** friendship with Alain Ducasse; primacy in Franco-Peruvian fusion; celebrity or political clientele; anything about his life after 1998; and any review in the **French** press, from any publication, in any year.

### On the clientele claim specifically

Repeated AI searches have returned confident paragraphs naming Fujimori, Brad Pitt and Jennifer Aniston **with no citations attached**. That is prompt-echo, not confirmation, and this dossier does not record it as fact.

That said, the user reports first-hand confirmation from Christian himself and a former waitress. **Oral testimony from named participants is legitimate historical evidence** — it simply has to be captured properly to be citable. A protocol for doing that, including the critical rule of asking open questions *before* naming any names, is in [`07`](dossier/07_open_questions_and_leads.md) Lead 3.3.
