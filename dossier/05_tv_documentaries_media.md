# 05 — Television, Documentaries and Media

## The central finding of this dossier

**In 1997, while running the kitchen of a Michelin-starred Paris restaurant, Christian Conticini co-fronted a 26-part national television series on the science of cooking, opposite Hervé This.**

This is the most substantial documented public activity located for Christian Conticini anywhere in this research, and it had been misfiled in earlier work as a print recipe series.

### Why this matters more than it first appears

**1. It puts Christian in the room where molecular gastronomy was being made public.** Hervé This is not a television personality who happens to like food. With the Oxford physicist **Nicholas Kurti**, he coined *molecular gastronomy* and founded the discipline. He is a research chemist at INRA and the intellectual origin point of a movement that reshaped high-end cooking worldwide from the late 1990s. To be the cook standing opposite him for 26 episodes, in 1997, is to be This's chosen practical counterpart at the moment the field went public in France.

**2. It is a genuine collaboration of equals in format.** The structure — chef cooks, scientist explains — requires the chef to be technically articulate and to work in a way that *exposes* the chemistry. This is not a chef being filmed. It is a chef being used as the demonstrative instrument of a scientific argument, 26 times.

**3. It corroborates the 1994 polemic.** Christian's 1994 attack on the cult of the "natural, authentic" product and his 1997 role explaining the chemistry of cooking on television are the **same intellectual position, three years apart**: cooking as transformation and technique rather than as the reverent presentation of pure ingredients. And his 1998 Libération recipes appear in the **"futurs"** (science/technology) section. Three independent traces, 1994–1998, all pointing the same way. In a dossier this thin on primary text, that internal consistency is the most persuasive evidence available about who this cook actually was.

**4. It dates his public profile precisely.** 1997, national broadcast, while still at the stove at Anvers.

### The orthography problem

The brief writes **"Toqués à la loupe"**; the producer's URL slug is **`toques-a-la-loupe`**.

- *La toque* = the chef's hat, hence by metonymy the chef. *"Toques à la loupe"* = "chefs under the magnifying glass."
- *Toqué* = crazy, cracked. *"Toqués à la loupe"* = "crackpots under the magnifying glass," with a pun on *toque*.

Both are plausible French title puns, and URL slugs routinely strip accents, so the slug is not decisive. **Unresolved.** Anyone searching INA or the BnF should try **both spellings**, and also *"Toques à la loupe"* with and without the accent, before concluding a record does not exist.

## §3 Retrieval routes for the audiovisual material

These are the practical steps, for a human.

### INA (Institut national de l'audiovisuel)

INA holds French broadcast legal deposit. La Cinquième broadcasts of 1997 fall squarely within it.

1. **inatheque.fr / the INA thèque catalogue** — search **both** `"Toques à la loupe"` and `"Toqués à la loupe"`; also `Tourancheau Toques`; also `"Hervé This" 1997 La Cinquième`; also individual episode titles, e.g. `"Ceviche de langoustine"`.
2. **institut.ina.fr / madelen** — the public-facing archive; some material is streamable.
3. **On-site consultation** at the **INAthèque**, housed at the BnF site François-Mitterrand, Paris. Broadcast legal deposit is consultable on site by accredited researchers, generally free. **This is the realistic route to actually watching the series**, and it is the single highest-value physical-archive action in the whole dossier.
4. **INA media sales** (inamediapro.com) for licensed copies.

### BnF audiovisual department

1. Retrieve `ark:/12148/cb444579018` and `cb444578192`; note the material designation and shelf mark.
2. Retrieve `catalogue.bnf.fr/liste-de-notices.do?arkNotice=ark:/12148/cb450830921` — if this is the collection-level record, it should list all 26 episodes at once.
3. From the author-refinement URL (`affinerAut.do?...index0=AUT3;12114908`), export the **complete** attached-records list. That closes the episode inventory.

### The producer and the director

1. **Agat Films & Cie / Ex Nihilo** — an active, reputable Paris documentary house. Their catalogue page for the series exists. A direct enquiry about a 1997 title, its episode list and any surviving materials is a reasonable and low-cost approach.
2. **Doc & Film International** — named as distributor; may hold a sales sheet with the full episode list.
3. **Philippe Tourancheau**, the director, is a working French documentarian. He is a living witness to 26 days-plus of filming with Christian Conticini. **This may be the most valuable single interview available to anyone pursuing this subject** — a first-hand professional account of Christian at work, from someone with no stake in the Philippe narrative.

### Hervé This

Hervé This is alive, active, publishes prolifically, and has written extensively about the history of molecular gastronomy. He worked alongside Christian across 26 episodes.

- Search his **published bibliography and blog** for mentions of the series and of Conticini.
- **A direct enquiry is entirely reasonable.** He is known to be accessible to serious questions.
- His own account would be a **first-hand primary source on Christian's technical ability and thinking** — and, unlike almost everything else in this dossier, it would be new evidence rather than recovered evidence.
