# 01 — Biography and Chronology

**Scope discipline:** this is a biography of **Christian** Conticini. Philippe enters only where he bears directly on Christian.

**Evidence rules applied throughout:**
- **[VERIFIED]** — corroborated by multiple independent indexed sources, internally consistent.
- **[PARTIAL]** — attested by an indexed summary of a source the agent could not read in original form.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** — asserted somewhere (including by the commissioning user), no source located.
- **[COULD-NOT-ACCESS]** — a specific document that would settle the point, which the sandbox could not reach.

Every section separates **primary-source facts** from **inference**.

---

## §1 Family and origins

### Facts

- Christian Conticini is the **brother of Philippe Conticini** (b. 1963), the pastry chef. **[VERIFIED]**
- The family is a restaurant family. The father is named **Roger Conticini**. **[PARTIAL]** — from the indexed summary of the L'Express article of May 1993, *"La maison Conticini, de fils en père."*
- That article's substance, as indexed: contrary to the usual pattern in which sons leave fathers, **Roger first helped his two sons make La Table d'Anvers one of the recognised tables of Paris, and only then left to open his own house** — a bistro near the Champ-de-Mars (7th arrondissement), described in terms of an old-style *cantine* with *plats canailles* and good small wines. **[PARTIAL]**
  - **Note on addresses:** the Champ-de-Mars bistro is **Roger's own separate establishment**. La Table d'Anvers is elsewhere, at 2 place d'Anvers in the 9th arrondissement. The two must not be conflated.

### Inference (clearly marked as such)

The headline *"de fils en père"* is a deliberate inversion of the idiom *de père en fils* ("from father to son"). The joke is the whole thesis of the piece: transmission ran **upward**. If that is right, then by 1993 the sons — Christian among them — were the established professionals and the father the newcomer. That is a meaningful data point about Christian's standing at that date. **This is inference from a headline and an indexed summary; the article body has not been read.**

### Not established

- Christian's **date and place of birth** — not found. Philippe's 1963 birth year is well documented; Christian's is not. Whether Christian is the elder or younger brother is **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- The family's Italian-descended surname invites speculation about origins. **No source found. Not asserted here.**

## §2 Training and formation (pre-1986)

### Facts

**None established.** This is the largest single gap in the dossier.

### The Martinez claim

The brief asks about training at the **Hôtel Martinez, Cannes**. Status: **[UNVERIFIED]**.

Searches on "Conticini" + "Martinez" return, overwhelmingly, **Christian Sinicropi** — the two-star chef of the Palme d'Or at the Hôtel Martinez. The shared forename "Christian" plus the Martinez association produces a textbook **name-collision false positive**. Any prior "confirmation" of this claim should be checked for exactly this contamination before being believed.

This does not disprove the claim. Kitchen apprenticeships of the 1970s–early 1980s are almost never documented online. It means the claim needs a documentary source: a *Who's Who*-style culinary directory entry, a period profile interview, or the Martinez's own staff records.

### Inference

For a chef to open a restaurant in 1986 that took a Michelin star, a serious brigade formation in the preceding decade is close to a professional necessity. That is a structural expectation, not a fact, and it points to *some* prestigious house — not specifically to the Martinez.

## §3 1986 — La Table d'Anvers opens

### Facts

- **La Table d'Anvers opened in 1986** at **2 place d'Anvers, 75009 Paris**, at the foot of Montmartre. **[VERIFIED]** for year and location. The brief specifies **May 1986**; the month is **[PARTIAL]** — no consulted source independently confirms the month.
- It was founded by **the two Conticini brothers together**. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Division of labour: Christian ran the savoury kitchen (*la partie salée* / *la cuisine*); Philippe ran pastry.** **[VERIFIED]** — this is stated consistently by every source that touches the restaurant, including sources otherwise written entirely about Philippe.

### Why the division of labour matters

It is the load-bearing fact of the whole dossier. Christian is not a silent partner, a manager, or a financier. He is *the chef of the savoury food* at a Michelin-starred restaurant. Any claim the restaurant made in cooking — technique, sourcing, fusion, menu structure — is, on the savoury side, **his** claim. The historiographical problem is simply that Philippe's later fame has absorbed the restaurant's entire record into a Philippe narrative.

### Inference

The 9th arrondissement at the foot of Montmartre was not, in 1986, a luxury-gastronomy district. Opening an ambitious restaurant there rather than in the 7th, 8th or 16th reads as a deliberately unconventional siting decision. **Inference only** — no source states a rationale.

## §4 1986–1998 — the Table d'Anvers years

### Facts

- The restaurant obtained **one Michelin star** and **17/20 in Gault & Millau**. **[PARTIAL]** — universally repeated, never sourced to a guide edition. See `03_michelin_gaultmillau_criticism.md`.
- It became a **notable address of contemporary Parisian gastronomy**. **[PARTIAL]**
- **Philippe was named Gault & Millau Pâtissier of the Year in 1991** — an award to Philippe, recorded here only because it dates the restaurant's peak visibility. **[PARTIAL]**
- **Verrine desserts are dated to 1994 at La Table d'Anvers** and credited to Philippe. **[PARTIAL]** — Philippe's innovation, not Christian's; noted for chronology only, and the dossier does not pursue it.

### Dated Christian-specific events within this period

The 1998 headline is itself evidentiary: *"détaille quelques recettes de son cru"* — "sets out several recipes of his own." A newspaper introducing a chef that way in 1998 treats him as a recognised cook with an authorial voice, and the phrasing ("de son cru") signals original creations rather than reportage.

### The 1989 book

*La Cuisine gourmande des stars*, 1989, ~50 pages, illustrated, appears in BnF listings. **Attribution between Christian and Philippe is unresolved** — one BnF-derived listing credits Christian, another associates Philippe. **[PARTIAL]**, flagged as contested. ## §5 1997 — the television year

This is treated in full in `05_tv_documentaries_media.md`. In summary:

- **1997**: *Toques à la loupe*, 26 × ~13 min, dir. **Philippe Tourancheau**, prod. **Agat Films & Cie / Ex Nihilo**, broadcast **La Cinquième**. Christian Conticini cooks; **Hervé This** supplies the physical chemistry. Each episode takes one dish and opens up the chemistry inside it. **[PARTIAL→strong]** — attested by the producer's own catalogue entry and by BnF audiovisual notices, both via indexed summary.

Known/likely episode titles, taken from the work-titles attached to Christian's BnF authority record: *Quenelles de brochet · Morue, œuf gras cuit · Salade de thon · Crème de potiron · Pigeons aux potimarrons · Ceviche de langoustine · Épaule d'agneau rôtie · Croquettes au chocolat · Monte Christo · Saint-Jacques poêlées · Fricassée de volaille · Tarte aux poireaux · Saumon aux lentilles.* Thirteen of a stated twenty-six. **[PARTIAL]**

**Correction on the record:** these titles were previously read as printed recipes co-authored with Hervé This. The BnF notice for *Morue, œuf gras cuit* (ark:/12148/cb444579018) carries a **"Philippe Tourancheau réal."** credit — a director credit, hence an audiovisual record. They are episodes.

## §6 1998 and after

### Facts

- **Philippe left the restaurant in 1998**, going on to Petrossian and an international career. **[PARTIAL]**
- **La Table d'Anvers did not long survive that departure**; the address later became *Le Grand Comptoir d'Anvers*. **[PARTIAL]** — no source indicates an abrupt or contentious closure; the indexed accounts describe an ordinary wind-down.
- **Christian's subsequent career is not documented in any accessible source.** **[UNVERIFIED]** — searches return only "very little public information on Christian's independent or current path," with the observation that he appears to have continued more discreetly, outside his brother's media orbit.

### Inference

The asymmetry is itself the finding. A chef who held a Michelin star for roughly a decade, published in a national daily, and fronted a national television series has effectively no post-1998 digital footprint. Two readings are available and the evidence does not choose between them: either he withdrew from public cooking, or he continued in roles that generate no press. **Neither is established.**
