# 04 — Books, Recipes, and the BnF Bibliography

## The headline correction

**The "1997 recipe series co-authored with Hervé This" does not exist as a printed work. Those BnF records are television episodes.**

Earlier research read the work-titles attached to Christian Conticini's BnF authority record — *Ceviche de langoustine*, *Morue, œuf gras cuit*, *Pigeons aux potimarrons*, and so on — as a co-authored 1997 recipe series, and drew conclusions about him as a culinary-science *author*.

The BnF notice for one of those exact ARKs (`cb444579018`, *Morue, œuf gras cuit*) carries the statement of responsibility **"Philippe Tourancheau réal."** — *réalisateur*, director. That is an audiovisual record. Cross-referenced with the Agat Films catalogue, every one of these titles is an episode of the 1997 television series ***Toques à la loupe*** (26 × 13 min, dir. Tourancheau, La Cinquième), in which Christian Conticini cooks and Hervé This explains the chemistry.

So Christian's BnF "works" of 1997 are **audiovisual legal-deposit records for individual television episodes**, catalogued one by one. Full treatment in `05_tv_documentaries_media.md`.

**Consequences:**
- The printed bibliography is **much shorter** than previously supposed — plausibly a single contested title.
- The audiovisual filmography is **much larger and better documented** than previously supposed.
- Retrieval moves from library stacks to **INA and BnF audiovisual deposit**.
- The phrase "co-authored with Hervé This" should be replaced by "appeared with Hervé This."

## §2 Printed works

### *La Cuisine gourmande des stars* (1989)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Title | *La Cuisine gourmande des stars* |
| Year | 1989 |
| Extent | c. 50 pp., illustrated |
| Attribution | **CONTESTED** |
| Tag | **[PARTIAL]**, with attribution flagged unresolved |

**The attribution problem, stated plainly.** One BnF-derived listing credits this title to **Christian**; another associates it with **Philippe**. Indexed retrieval confirms it appears among the works listed for Christian on data.bnf.fr and dates it to 1989. That does not settle authorship: BnF work-listings can attach a title to a person through any contributor role, and a jointly-produced book by two brothers running one restaurant is exactly the case where cataloguing gets muddled.

**Do not state flatly that Christian wrote this book.** State: *"A 1989 title, La Cuisine gourmande des stars, appears in BnF listings associated with Christian Conticini; attribution between the two brothers is unresolved."*

**How to settle it:** obtain the full BnF notice and read the **statement of responsibility** as printed on the title page (BnF transcribes it), plus the ISBN and publisher. Then, ideally, examine a physical copy — the title page is decisive. Second-hand copies of an illustrated 1989 French cookery title are likely findable on abebooks.fr, priceminister/Rakuten, or leboncoin.

**On the title itself.** *"La cuisine gourmande des stars"* — celebrity/gourmet cooking — reads as a popular illustrated trade title rather than a chef's manifesto. Fifty pages is a booklet. Whoever wrote it, it should not be treated as a major statement of culinary philosophy. **Inference from title and extent only.**

### Any other printed works

**None located.** No other book, no printed recipe collection, no contribution to a collective volume was found attached to Christian.

This is itself worth recording. For a starred chef of the 1986–1998 period, publishing a cookbook was close to standard practice. Christian appears essentially **not to have done so** — which is consistent with the broader pattern of a chef with a substantial professional record and a minimal publishing footprint, and with a brother who absorbed the family's publishing output entirely.

## §3 Journalism — the recipes that do exist in print

Christian's genuine printed recipe output that this research has located is **journalistic, in *Libération***:

| Date | Title | Nature | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **18 April 1998** | *"Savez-vous manger les choux ? Christian Conticini détaille quelques recettes de son cru"* | **Recipes** — original, by-lined | [PARTIAL] |
| **12 July 1994** | Untitled here; attacks the cult of the "sain, authentique et naturel" product; Libération *Rebonds* | Opinion piece | [PARTIAL] |

The 1998 headline's phrase **"quelques recettes de son cru"** — *"several recipes of his own devising"* — is the only located instance of a publication presenting Christian as an originating recipe author. **The recipes themselves have not been read.**

**On the supposed recurring column:** two by-lined pieces four years apart is compatible with a column but does not evidence one. **[UNVERIFIED].** See `07`, lead #3, for the enumeration method.

**A recurring hazard, restated:** the 1998 piece is about ***le chou***, the cabbage. Searching it reliably returns ***la pâte à choux***, Philippe's choux pastry. Different word, different person, different subject. Guard against it.

## §4 The 1997 filmography, listed as bibliography for cross-reference

These are the titles attached to Christian's BnF record for 1997. **They are episodes, not publications.** Listed here so that anyone arriving at this dossier with the old "recipe series" understanding finds the correction at the point of confusion.

1. Quenelles de brochet
2. Morue, œuf gras cuit — *BnF ark:/12148/cb444579018, "Philippe Tourancheau réal."*
3. Salade de thon
4. Crème de potiron
5. Pigeons aux potimarrons
6. **Ceviche de langoustine**
7. Épaule d'agneau rôtie
8. Croquettes au chocolat
9. Monte Christo
10. Saint-Jacques poêlées
11. Fricassée de volaille
12. Tarte aux poireaux
13. Saumon aux lentilles

**Thirteen titles; the series is stated to have twenty-six episodes.** Roughly half the episode list is therefore missing from the material recovered here. Whether the remaining thirteen exist as separate BnF records (likely, given the batch-numbered ARKs) or under a series-level record (possibly `cb450830921`) is unresolved. **[PARTIAL]**

**Reading the list as evidence about Christian's cooking.** This is the closest thing we have to a menu, and it is worth reading as one — with the caveat that these are dishes chosen for a *pedagogical* series about food chemistry, so they may have been selected to illustrate reactions rather than to represent his restaurant. With that said:

- **Range:** river fish (quenelles de brochet), salt cod, tuna, langoustine, scallops, salmon; pigeon, lamb shoulder, poultry; vegetables (potiron, potimarron, poireaux); chocolate. Broad, savoury-led, classically grounded.
- **French classicism is dominant.** Quenelles de brochet is a Lyonnais monument. Morue, épaule d'agneau rôtie, fricassée de volaille, tarte aux poireaux, saint-jacques poêlées — this is the French repertoire.
- **One clear outlier: *Ceviche de langoustine*.** A Peruvian technique on a French luxury crustacean. In a list otherwise this classical, it stands out sharply, and its presence is not accidental.
- **Two puzzles.** *Monte Christo* is ambiguous — a sandwich, a cake, or something else. *Croquettes au chocolat* is a sweet item in a savoury chef's list, worth noting given the brother next door.
- **Curiosity of technique:** *"Morue, œuf gras cuit"* — the phrase *œuf gras cuit* is unusual and suggests a deliberate egg-cookery point, which is very much Hervé This's territory. Low-temperature egg cookery was one of This's signature demonstrations.

**All of the above is inference from titles.** Nobody involved in this dossier has watched an episode.
